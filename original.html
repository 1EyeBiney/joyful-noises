How the original album came together

This did not begin with a plan to make an album. “Be The Way” had already grown out of the podcast and the direction of our church. When I began trying the ChatGPT Desktop app with Suno, I wanted to see whether I could describe a spiritual idea and a musical sound, then have GPT help me turn those ideas into a song and operate a website that was difficult for me to use with a screen reader.

The album also reflects what we had been learning together at Hope Christian Church. Sunday messages had been returning to uncertainty, renewed thinking, obedience, the life and power of Jesus, and faith made visible in ordinary life. In our Thursday Bible study, we had been moving through Acts at roughly one chapter each week. Those studies kept placing real people in front of us who prayed, listened, followed, served, and praised God under pressure. The songs are not summaries of individual sermons or chapters, but they grew in soil those months of teaching had prepared.

“Before the Morning” was the first real follow-up. It gave words to the place I was in: I had received news that made my future path feel uncertain and threatened work I care deeply about—helping blind people in Indiana use technology and discover that a meaningful life after vision loss is still possible. I could not see very far down the road. I remembered Paul and Silas singing in prison before the doors opened. I knew I needed a different way to handle moments like this, and the song began with the decision to praise and trust that God was already present before I could see an answer.

From there, one song suggested another. Some began with a spiritual need. Others began as an experiment with a different branch of electronic music. As the night continued, the themes started answering one another: God is present, God does not change, nothing separates us from His love, Christ is our life, His hands still hold us, His voice can be heard through the noise, and we can be still because He is God. When we reached “Be Still,” I first recognized that these experiments might be becoming an album. By the time I copied the songs into a numbered folder at about 3:30 in the morning, the collection had taken shape.

The next night helped reveal what the collection was about. “You Are Calling” gave the album a moment of response: God is not only present; He calls us to follow when we cannot see the entire road. “A Joyful Noise” gave the collection its title and destination. I am hesitant to sing loudly because I do not enjoy hearing my own voice. These generated voices became one way for me to make the joyful noise I wished I could hear from myself. More deeply, the song expressed the choice to praise while my circumstances still gave me reasons to feel uncertain.
