The story behind the music

About A Series of Joyful Noises

When the next step is all you can see

This collection began after Brian received crushing news that could make it significantly harder for him to serve blind people in Indiana. He has devoted his career to sharing a lifetime of computer experience with people now facing the same loss of vision he has faced. After building a productive and meaningful life without sight, he wants others making that transition to know that purpose is still possible.

The news raised doubts about the road ahead. Doors seemed to be closing, the future was unclear, and only the next step was visible. So Brian tried to practice what Hope Christian Church had been teaching: seek the Word, praise when you are unsure, and talk with the Father. Through it, he has been reminded that God cares far more about who he is than what he does.

Why call it a joyful noise?

Brian does not think of himself as a good singer and is often hesitant to sing loudly. These songs became his way of making a joyful noise: a way to hear praise expressed with the joy and beauty he hears inwardly, especially when circumstances give him little reason to feel joyful.

Paul and Silas singing in prison helped shape the collection. So did the call to let Jesus teach us to pray, the assurance that God is always near, and the desire to point people toward Christ. “A Joyful Noise” closes both editions because living hope is the word Brian wants people to carry with them.

Why two editions?

The Original Electronic Edition reflects the trance, progressive electronic, and breakbeat music Brian loves. The Acoustic and Worship Edition carries most of the same ideas in arrangements that are easier for a wider audience to enter and sing. One gets you up and dancing; the other is for relaxing, reflecting, and joining in.

How Brian and GPT made the songs

Brian described the spiritual idea, musical direction, emotional shape, and sound he wanted. GPT helped connect themes to Scripture, develop concepts, and draft lyrics. Brian then edited and rewrote the words for nearly every song—changing rhyme, syllables, phrases, and sometimes whole sections—before listening and deciding what belonged.

The ChatGPT Desktop app also operated Suno through its website after Brian and GPT chose the song parameters. That mattered because the Suno site can be difficult to use with a screen reader. It is an example of AI doing more than generating material: it helped bridge an inaccessible interface while Brian remained the listener, editor, and final decision-maker.

Hope Christian Church and Be The Way

Many songs echo themes from recent messages and Bible studies at Hope Christian Church on Senour Road in Indianapolis. “Be The Way” began as promotional music for the church’s growing weekly podcast and eventually helped open the door to both albums.
